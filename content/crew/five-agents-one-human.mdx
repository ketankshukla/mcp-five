This is the part that stops it being a toy, and it is where the real
engineering is.

## The assumption you didn't know you'd made

Project #3's approval model has an assumption buried in it so obviously true
that it is invisible:

<Callout type="quote">
  The agent that hit the gate is the agent you are talking to.
</Callout>

The loop stops, the request ends, the browser shows a card, you click, a new
request restarts *that same loop*.

Delegation destroys it completely. The agent that hits the gate is:

- two levels down,
- running inside a `Promise.all`,
- alongside two siblings that are still working,
- with nobody talking to it and no browser tab of its own.

And if each worker asked separately, you would get three cards at three
different moments, out of order, each with no context.

<Callout type="lesson" title="Five agents each interrupting you is worse than no delegation at all">
  You delegated to stop having to hold the details. An approval flow that hands
  you three context-free decisions has handed the details straight back — and
  now you are making them under time pressure, out of order.
</Callout>

## Three details, and each one is a bug if you skip it

| The detail | What breaks without it |
|---|---|
| **`origin` on every bubbled call** | The card says "approve `empty_jar {id:38}`?" and you have no idea which of three workers wants it or what it found. You would be rubber-stamping decisions you cannot see — **worse than not asking, because it looks like oversight.** |
| **`partial_results`** | Clicking Approve re-runs the two workers that had already finished. You pay for them twice, they may answer differently the second time, and any side effect happens again. |
| **`paused_children`** | The stopped worker's entire conversation vanishes when the HTTP request ends, and there is nothing left to say yes *to*. |

And the thing that makes all of it work is the same thing that made [project
#3's pause](/learn/gate/pausing-is-an-array) work:

<Callout type="quote">
  The agent's whole state is its `messages` array. Persisting three agents is
  persisting three arrays.
</Callout>

No continuation, no serialised generator, no coroutine library. The freeze is a
`return` and the thaw is a function call with a bigger array — three times over
instead of once.

## The screen

That single approval standing in front of three agents' worth of work is the
picture the whole project is aiming at:

<Terminal>
{`══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  ⏸  ONE APPROVAL, 9 DESTRUCTIVE CALL(S)
     9 of them came from 3 sub-agent(s) that are frozen mid-task.
     Frozen sub-agents: "jars 1-20", "jars 21-40", "jars 41-60"
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

  ⏸  pantry__empty_jar  {"id":46,"reason":"Seal broken (lid gasket …"}
      asked by : sub-agent "jars 41-60"
      because  : Seal broken (lid gasket displaced) - not merely scuffed,
                 no AUTHORIZED note present.
      host rule: empty_jar permanently destroys every cookie in that jar.`}
</Terminal>

Nine calls. Three frozen agents. **One decision.**

Note the three lines under each call. `asked by` is `origin`. `because` is the
worker's own reasoning, carried up. `host rule` is the gate's `reason` from
[project #3's rule list](/learn/gate/rules-read-arguments). Together they are
enough to decide without going and reading three traces.

## And it survives a partial no

Eight were approved and the ninth denied. The worker that got the "no" read it
as an ordinary tool result, adapted, and reported honestly — and the
orchestrator **surfaced it to the human** rather than quietly dropping it:

<Callout type="quote">
  One jar flagged but NOT emptied: Jar 59 — it does meet the tampering
  criteria, but the sub-agent's attempt to empty it was declined. This one
  still needs your attention.
</Callout>

<Callout type="lesson" title="Verified against the database, not against the model's summary">
  8 jars emptied, jar 59 untouched, zero false positives.

  That distinction matters more than it sounds. The model's summary is a claim
  about what happened. The `jar_events` table is what happened. When you are
  checking whether an agent did the right thing, check the state — a
  well-written summary of the wrong actions is the most convincing failure mode
  there is.
</Callout>

## Rewinding a tree came free

Nested replay was the *stretch* goal. It cost one extra `select` and one
expandable row — because of a decision in `lib/run-driver.ts`: **a sub-agent
gets a real row in `runs`**, with a `parent_run_id`, and its own stream of
`trace_events` under its own sequence numbers.

It is not a special kind of thing needing special handling. It is a **run with
a parent**. So it already had a status, a stop reason, an iteration count and a
token bill, for the same reason every other run does.

<Callout type="insight" title="One deliberate UI choice">
  Each worker's trace is **collapsed by default**, and lazy-loaded.

  Delegation exists so the details of twenty jar inspections do not have to be
  in anybody's head — not the orchestrator's, and not yours. The summary is
  what you normally need. The full trace is there for the ten percent of the
  time when "trust me" isn't good enough.
</Callout>
