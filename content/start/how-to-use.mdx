## Read it in order

The sidebar is the order. It crosses section boundaries, and every page has a
prev/next pager at the bottom, so you can start at the first page and finish
at the last without ever choosing what to read next.

You *can* jump. Section 6 is a reference and stands alone. But sections 1
through 5 are a sequence — each one opens by breaking something the previous
one assumed, and that only lands if you were there when the assumption was
made.

| If you are… | Start at |
|---|---|
| new to MCP entirely | [What is MCP?](/learn/start/what-is-mcp) — you are nearly there |
| curious what Claude Desktop is actually doing | [🔁 The Host](/learn/host/a-while-loop) |
| about to give an agent a dangerous tool | [✋ The Gate](/learn/gate/hint-not-permission) |
| watching an agent run out of room on big jobs | [👥 The Crew](/learn/crew/the-ceiling) |
| worried something you connected can spend money | [💸 The Ledger](/learn/ledger/four-unused) |
| here for the war stories | [The gotcha compendium](/learn/picture/gotchas) |

## Every claim here has a number behind it

This is the property that makes the course worth your time, so it is worth
saying plainly.

**Every figure on this site came from a real run.** 439k tokens. 0 of 36. 13/13
for `$0.00`. 2.01× becoming 1.08×. None of them are illustrative, rounded for
effect, or invented to make a point land harder.

<Callout type="lesson" title="Which cuts both ways">
  Where a number is soft, this site says so. Project #4's 60-jar cost
  comparison is *"a wash"* rather than a percentage, because the run-to-run
  spread was a meaningful fraction of the gap. The 240-jar figures are one
  attempt per mode, so treat the magnitudes as one significant figure.

  A course that only reports its clean measurements is teaching you a genre,
  not a practice.
</Callout>

## The three-document convention

All five repos have the same three files, and the split is deliberate enough
to steal:

| File | For | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| **`README.md`** | Understanding | *What is this? Why does it exist?* |
| **`BUILD_FROM_SCRATCH.md`** | Doing | *Which commands, in what order, and what breaks?* |
| **`NEXT_STEP.md`** | Deciding | *What's missing, and what should the next project be?* |

The reason it works is that the three have genuinely different readers, and
each stays useful because it is not trying to serve the other two.

Every `BUILD_FROM_SCRATCH.md` ends with an appendix titled *"the N things that
actually broke."* There are **40** of them across the five projects, and they
are collected — searchable, filterable, with the cause and the fix — in [the
gotcha compendium](/learn/picture/gotchas).

## Two conventions on this site

**The section colour tells you where you are.** Each of the six sections has
one vivid accent, and it shows up on the headings, the active sidebar item, the
rule at the top of the page, and the strokes in the diagrams. If the page is
rose, you are in the Gate.

**The diagrams are live.** They are rendered in your browser from Mermaid
source, not shipped as images, so they scroll inside their own box rather than
blowing out the page. Most of them are lifted directly from the source READMEs
where the per-node colours already carry meaning — red is failure, green is
success, amber is the interesting bit.
